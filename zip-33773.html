Who lives here

ZIP 33773 skews older than both Florida and the country. The median resident is about 47, compared with a national median a little under 39. In the city of Largo as a whole, 28 percent of residents are 65 or older, and the city counts more than 6,000 veterans. That age profile explains why so many households here are on fixed incomes, why homestead and senior exemptions matter so much, and why special-needs evacuation registration deserves more attention than it gets.

Homes and ownership

Roughly three in four occupied homes are owner-occupied, well above the national rate. Housing is a mix of 1960s–1980s single-family homes, villa and condo communities, and manufactured-home parks. Many of the single-family homes predate Florida's 2002 statewide building code, which is exactly the profile the My Safe Florida Home program was built for.

Owner-reported median value in the census survey is $319,400. Market data moves faster: Redfin reported a median sale price near $386,000 in mid-2026, with homes selling in about 40 days. Treat market medians as a snapshot, since a handful of lakefront sales can swing them.

Risk: wind matters more than you think

Redfin, using First Street climate models, rates flood risk in 33773 as moderate, affecting about 44 percent of properties, and wind risk as extreme. That pairing is the key insight for this ZIP. Most of 33773 sits on higher ground than the beaches, so storm surge is a smaller threat than in Zone A neighborhoods. But rainfall flooding along creeks and lakes, and wind damage to older roofs, are real. Hurricane Milton showed both in 2024: 12 to 15 inches of rain, overflowing creeks, and widespread tree and roof damage.

Practical takeaway: in 33773, roof age, opening protection and a flood policy usually matter more to your finances than which evacuation zone you're in.
