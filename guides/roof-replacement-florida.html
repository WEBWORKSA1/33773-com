Roofs and insurance in Florida

Reviewed October 2026 by the 33773 Homefront editors

In Florida your roof decides your insurance more than any other part of your house. Plan its replacement on your schedule, not your insurer's.

The roof-age rules

  • Under a 2022 Florida law, an insurer may not refuse to write or renew a policy solely because of roof age if the roof is less than 15 years old.
  • For roofs 15 years or older, the homeowner can have the roof inspected. If the inspection shows at least five years of useful life remaining, the insurer may not refuse coverage based on age alone.
  • Insurers can still decline for actual condition, damage or other underwriting reasons, and can price older roofs higher.

The two inspections that matter

InspectionWhat it showsWhen you need it
Four-pointRoof, electrical, plumbing and HVAC condition and ageUsually required by insurers for older homes (often 25+ or 30+ years)
Wind mitigationRoof shape, deck attachment, roof-to-wall connections, secondary water barrier, opening protectionAny time; it earns premium credits

The 25 percent rule

Florida's building code historically required that if more than 25 percent of a roof section was repaired or replaced within 12 months, the whole section had to be brought to current code. Since 2022, if your existing roof was built, repaired or replaced to the 2007 Florida Building Code or later, only the damaged portion needs to meet current code. This can make storm repairs cheaper for newer roofs. Older roofs may still trigger a full replacement.

Planning a replacement

  1. Get your current roof's permit history from the City of Largo or Pinellas County, depending on whether your address is incorporated.
  2. Collect three written quotes from licensed roofing contractors. Confirm licences on the DBPR licence search.
  3. Ask each to include a secondary water barrier, roof deck re-nailing to current code and upgraded roof-to-wall connections where accessible. These earn insurance credits.
  4. Check whether you qualify for My Safe Florida Home before you sign.
  5. Schedule outside peak hurricane months if you can. After a storm, crews and prices tighten for months.
  6. Get a new wind-mitigation inspection after completion and send it to your insurer.
