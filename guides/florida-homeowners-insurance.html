Florida homeowners insurance, explained for Largo owners

Reviewed October 2026 by the 33773 Homefront editors

For many 33773 households, insurance is now the fastest-growing part of the cost of owning a home. Understanding what an insurer is actually pricing is the first step to paying less.

Why premiums are so high

Florida carries more hurricane exposure than any other state, and reinsurance, the insurance that insurers buy, is priced globally after every major catastrophe. Years of litigation costs, several insurer insolvencies and back-to-back storms in 2022 and 2024 pushed rates up sharply. The state passed reforms in 2022 and 2023 aimed at reducing litigation and attracting carriers back; results vary by home and carrier, which is why shopping matters.

Citizens Property Insurance

Citizens is Florida's state-created insurer of last resort. It grew rapidly as private carriers pulled back, and the state now actively moves policies to private carriers through a takeout ("depopulation") process. If you receive a takeout offer, compare it carefully: coverage, deductibles and the carrier's financial strength rating all matter, not just price. Citizens also has eligibility rules that can require you to accept a comparable private offer, so read any notice closely and ask your agent.

Seven ways to lower your premium

  1. Get a wind-mitigation inspection. A licensed inspector documents roof shape, deck attachment, roof-to-wall connection, secondary water barrier and opening protection on the state's form. Insurers are required to offer credits for qualifying features. Inspections typically cost around $100–$150, and the free My Safe Florida Home inspection counts if you qualify.
  2. Replace or recertify an aging roof before renewal, not after a non-renewal letter.
  3. Add opening protection. Impact-rated windows and garage doors often deliver the largest credits after the roof.
  4. Raise your deductible if you have savings to cover it. Run the numbers both ways.
  5. Shop through an independent agent who can quote several carriers at once.
  6. Bundle home, auto and umbrella where the carrier offers it.
  7. Install monitored alarms and water-leak sensors, which some carriers credit.

Coverage checks people miss

  • Flood is never in a homeowners policy. See our flood guide.
  • Replacement cost vs actual cash value on your roof and contents: ACV pays depreciated value.
  • Ordinance or law coverage pays to bring a damaged home up to current code, which can be expensive for older Largo homes.
  • Additional living expenses cover hotel and meals if your home is uninhabitable.
  • Screen enclosures and pool cages are often excluded or capped for hurricane damage.
Complaints or disputes with an insurer can go to the Florida Department of Financial Services consumer helpline, which offers free mediation for some claims.
