Retiring in Largo: what 33773 is really like

Reviewed October 2026 by the 33773 Homefront editors

Largo has long drawn retirees: 28 percent of city residents are 65 or older. The appeal is real, and so are the costs that brochures leave out.

The appeal

  • No state income tax in Florida, which matters for pensions and retirement withdrawals.
  • Free green space: Lake Seminole Park, the Florida Botanical Gardens, Heritage Village and the Pinellas Trail are all close.
  • Established 55+ and villa communities, many with low-maintenance single-storey homes.
  • Healthcare: several hospitals and specialist networks are within a short drive across mid-Pinellas.

Exemptions for older residents

Beyond the standard homestead exemption, check the senior exemption for qualifying low-income residents 65 and older, and exemptions for veterans, widows and widowers, and people with disabilities. All are filed with the Pinellas County Property Appraiser by March 1.

Storm planning for older adults

  • Register with the special-needs evacuation program if you rely on oxygen, dialysis, powered equipment or need help evacuating.
  • Arrange a buddy: a neighbour or relative who checks in before and after every storm.
  • Keep two weeks of medication and a printed list of prescriptions and doctors.
  • Consider leaving earlier than the order. Heat and power outages are often more dangerous than wind for older adults.

Choosing housing

Manufactured-home communities are common and affordable, but they must evacuate for every storm order and are vulnerable to wind. Concrete-block single-family homes with a newer roof and impact openings are the most storm-resilient and insurable. Condos shift some insurance and maintenance to the association, so read reserve studies and recent assessments before buying.
